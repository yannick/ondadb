================================================================================
GATE DECISION
================================================================================

PASS, on three of the four acceptance criteria outright and on the fourth with a
stated caveat.

1. Categorical reduction on full coverage — PASS, by a wide margin.
   Deleting 60k keys key-by-key writes 1,010,178 bytes of tombstone debris and
   takes 14.6 s end to end. One `delete_range` covering the same keys writes 152
   bytes (three fragments plus framing) and takes 166 ms: 6,646x fewer bytes and
   87.8x less wall time. Both end at zero resident bytes, so this is the same
   work done, not less work done.

2. Delete latency p50/p99, with and without concurrent point writers — RECORDED,
   and it is the RV-M3 baseline the design asked for.
   Uncontended, a range commit costs 7.9 us at p50. With four threads writing
   points as fast as they can, that rises to 44.2 us — the `commit_mu`
   acquisition a range commit now takes at every isolation level, which is
   exactly the cost M3 names. It is worth being precise about what the p99
   column is NOT: at 11.9 ms (0 writers) and 22.7 ms (4 writers) it is dominated
   by flush and compaction stalls, not by the lock — the ordinary POINT commits
   measured in the same runs show a p99 of 7.0 ms and 28.5 ms respectively. Under
   load the range commit's p99 is BELOW the point commit's. So the honest
   statement is: range commits pay a real, measurable p50 penalty for the lock
   (5.6x under contention), and are not the tail-latency bottleneck.

3. Point/scan p99 with 0/10/1000 fragments — PASS.
       fragments   get p99    full scan
               0    0.96 us      1.6 ms
              10    1.08 us      2.7 ms
            1000    1.12 us      2.7 ms
   The claim that matters is 10 -> 1000: a hundredfold increase in fragments
   moves get p99 by 0.04 us and the scan not at all. That is the monotonic
   cursor and the one-fragment binary search behaving as designed; a per-key
   linear walk would have shown up here as a hundredfold difference.

   CAVEAT, stated rather than smoothed over: the 0-fragment column is not a
   clean control. It is a database that never enabled the capability, so its
   tables also use the LEGACY entry layout, while the 10- and 1000-fragment
   columns use the extended (kind-bearing) one. The 1.6 -> 2.7 ms scan step
   therefore mixes "no range mask" with "one extra uvarint per entry" — and the
   latter is 1.0-B's cost, not 1.2's. What 1.2 does own is proven separately and
   more strictly by `no_range_cf_allocates_nothing_on_read`, which asserts that a
   point-only column family materializes ZERO range-mask sources across 200
   gets, a forward scan, a reverse scan and a 200-key `multi_get`. Isolating the
   mask cost from the layout cost needs a fourth column (capability on, zero
   spans) and is left for the surfaces pass.

4. Time to space reclaim — PASS.
   9.66 s key-by-key versus 148 ms for the range delete, both to zero resident
   bytes: 65x.

Zero failed invariant or fault tests: `tests/range_delete.rs` (36 tests,
including the oracle over random histories and the level->=1 disjointness
property test) is green in both feature configurations, as is the rest of the
suite.

Methodology. Medians of 5 runs on an idle Apple M2 Max. This machine is

`docs/performance.md`), so only same-run ratios are interpreted above; the
